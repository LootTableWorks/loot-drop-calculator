The continuation method

Keep consequences, not a transcript

The next session does not need every name, roll, or room from the one-shot. It needs the few facts that changed what the players can do and what the world will do in response.

  1. 01

    Write the ending truth

    State one fact that cannot be reset without denying the final scene: the beacon was relit, the heir escaped, or the town chose a side.

    The world changed
  2. 02

    Name the changed relationship

    Choose the person or faction most affected by the party. Record the new posture as trust, fear, debt, suspicion, or open opposition.

    Someone responds
  3. 03

    Advance one pressure

    Move a clock the party could not fully stop. A rival acts, supplies run short, evidence spreads, or the weather closes a route.

    Delay has a cost
  4. 04

    Keep one promise unresolved

    Preserve a question the players already care about. It can be an obligation, a mystery, a threatened place, or an opportunity with a deadline.

    The next choice matters
  5. 05

    Ask what the players intend next

    Use their stated intention to frame the opening. Do not plan a route they have not chosen; prepare the pressure that meets the route they did.

Continuation questions

            
Keep only what changes play

          

          
The method is intentionally small: enough continuity to make the next session matter, without converting the one-shot into a transcript.

        

        

          

            
Does the one-shot need an unresolved ending to become a campaign?

            
No. A complete victory still changes relationships, advances pressures elsewhere, and creates obligations or opportunities that can open the next session.

          

          

            
How many facts should I keep after a one-shot?

            
Keep five: the ending truth, one changed relationship, one advanced pressure, one unresolved promise, and the players' next intention.

          

          

            
Does the Continuation Planner store or upload campaign notes?

            
No. The planner runs in the current browser page, does not use an account, and only copies or downloads a brief when you explicitly choose that action.
